# Q1 2026 — 13-week project-based robotics learning plan (SCARA-01)

**Updated:** 2026-10-26 · **Window:** Mon 2026-10-26 → Sun 2027-01-24 · **Budget:** ~65 focused hours (~5/week).

**Curriculum decision:** robotics engineering and ROS 2 are taught together against a single low-energy, desktop **SCARA-01 compact four-axis R-R-P-R** concept with a separate 3-D-printable gripper actuator. The teaching order is **physical problem → mechanics/math/control theory → calculation/experiment → ROS 2/software implementation → learner explanation/evidence**. Neither a ROS 2 software-only track nor theory without application is the main path.

**Authoritative project concept:** [SCARA-01 engineering brief](../projects/SCARA-01-CONCEPT.md). **Robot-learning-ready requirements:** [state/command/data/feedback interface](../projects/SCARA-01-LEARNING-PLATFORM.md) · [ADR-0002](../decisions/ADR-0002-scara-learning-platform.md).

## Q1 outcomes / mandatory exit gates

1. Define and justify SCARA-01's small-part tray-to-tray pick-and-place task, workcell layout, geometry, payload assumptions, motion limits and basic independent safety concepts.
2. Independently derive and numerically test 2R FK and IK (both branches, joint-limited/unreachable cases) and demonstrate SCARA R-R-P position model plus wrist-yaw relation. Test Jacobian/velocity or singularity cases.
3. Produce a parametric mechanical concept and quantified initial torque/speed/load/stiffness, measured-state actuator/encoder selection, gripper-feedback and future camera-mount tradeoffs; **no unvalidated purchase or promise of payload accuracy**.
4. Build a reproducible model and simulated R-R-P tray-to-tray sequence, incorporating independent wrist yaw J4 when feasible with URDF/Xacro, ROS 2 state/frame observation and recorded outcomes, or explicitly document simulator blockers with a reproducible kinematic fallback. Establish a clear pass/fail definition.
5. Document a **proposed** versioned ROS 2 command/observation interface and minimal time-stamped episode record (actual measured vs requested states, gripper and faults); no physical logger, images, dataset or trained policy required.
6. Produce a reviewed Q1 engineering report, learning evidence and a justified Q2 plan.

**Stretch only:** safely controlled low-voltage 2R physical benchtop prototype (potentially Z/gripper if justified). A fully built four-axis machine with gripper is **not** a Q1 commitment. Hardware purchasing and motion require appropriate mechanism calculations, guarding, limits, an independent stop and supervision. ROS 2 is not the sole safety mechanism.

Software is used as an engineering instrument *within* these tasks. Services/Actions, deeper QoS/DDS, Motion Planning/MoveIt 2 and the choice of low-level control interface are learned when their capabilities solve a SCARA-01 problem. Do not install infrastructure to complete a checklist. Vision AI and ERP integration are later expansions, not mandatory Q1 deliverables. **Q1 designs for future Physical AI** (real joint feedback, gripper state, bounded host commands, timestamped data, simulation parity and camera mounting) **but does not require a camera, dataset, learned policy or autonomous hardware motion**.

## Week 1 — adaptive plan (Oct 26–Nov 1)

**Primary objective (revised after learner feedback, 2026-10-26):** teach SCARA-01's motion architecture and joint-vs-task spaces as an integrated robotics problem. Earlier DoF, ideal workspace, collisions, gripper aperture and a simple static friction calculation were covered conceptually. **Do not continue into detailed gripper motor torque/rack-and-pinion sizing now.** Keep mechanical calculations on record for Week 5–6 design decisions, and prioritize motion, FK/IK, feedback and the observe–plan–execute–observe loop at a deliberate pace.

| Session | Budget | Topic | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A | 60 min | SCARA vs 2R vs 3R; choose compact R-R-P-R; roles of J1/J2/Z/wrist yaw and printed gripper; draw end-to-end pick/place | Explain 3 position coordinates + independent yaw, and why the gripper's actuation is separate |
| B | 75 min | Define compact tray geometry, representative 3-D-printed part, printed parallel gripper grasp, yaw and accuracy vs repeatability; note later grasp-state sensing | Requirements v0 with uncertainties and measurable test method |
| C | 75 min | Sketch compact 2R geometry and estimate ideal radial reach for provisional `L1=130 mm`, `L2=110 mm`; introduce joint limits, colliding gripper/links and printable stiffness | Labelled workcell and workspace calculation (20–240 mm ideal radial envelope, *not* usable reach); prior Place=(160,200) no longer within maximum |
| D | 60 min | Introduce **task space** (desired x,y,z,yaw) vs **joint space** (q1,q2,q3,q4), why IK is needed, and the motion chain goal → IK → path/trajectory → low-level control → measured feedback | Explain why a reachable tool pose may have more than one joint configuration; distinguish goal, trajectory, motor command and observed state |
| E | 30 min | One SCARA target-pose reasoning exercise (no motor selection); reflect on unknown joint limits, safe waypoints and how feedback closes the loop | Learner explains the goal-to-joints process in their own words; record gaps without implying mastered IK |

**Total:** 5 hours. These are flexible thematic slots, **not instructions to redo material already discussed**. Don't re-teach introductory 2D FK or rerun concurrency labs only to satisfy a checklist. The original A–C requirements remain engineering follow-ups where actual measurements are still missing; D is the immediate teaching emphasis.

### Learning checkpoint / teaching-depth decision (2026-10-26)

- Covered through dialogue: 4-DOF R-R-P-R concept and independent wrist yaw, compact printable gripper, simple ideal annular XY workspace, why tool/link collisions depend on configuration, safe Z lift-and-transfer concept, aperture margin and a **single** introductory two-finger friction calculation. Learner correctly recognized force doubles when contact friction coefficient halves. This is a **conceptual response**, not a measured gripper-force qualification.
- Learner requested a stronger focus on foundational **robot motion/controls and eventual Physical AI**. Detail-heavy mechanical component formulas (further gripping-force, gear/rack sizing, print design) are now **just-in-time Week 5–6 engineering material**, not the teaching sequence's driving topic. Do not repeat the friction example without a design reason.
- **Next teaching concept:** distinguish tool pose in Cartesian/task space from joint positions; analyze whether there can be multiple joint solutions for one pose, then introduce FK/IK with SCARA. One small example/check at a time, no rushed full-stack implementation.

### Learning checkpoint — motion and Jacobian (2026-10-26)

**Evidence:** [Dialogue-based SCARA motion/Jacobian note](../evidence/2026-10-26-scara-motion-jacobian-learning.md).

- **Correct learner reasoning:** a target pose can have multiple elbow configurations; Motion Planning considers collision-free paths, not just IK at the target; joint interpolation generally does not trace a Cartesian straight line; joint angular velocities generally must vary to achieve a commanded Cartesian velocity.
- **Tutor-introduced math/physics:** 2R Jacobian as partial derivatives of FK and a local joint-velocity → tool-velocity map; the numeric `q1=0°, q2=90°` example, determinant `L1*L2*sin(q2)`, straight-arm singularity, a control/Physical-AI motivation. The learner **reported these concepts understood**, but no independent Jacobian derivation, test log or SCARA motion code has been assessed.
- **Move at the learner's pace:** next a 35–45 min independent Jacobian calculation (one numeric example); then a 40–50 min IK branch vs safe path/trajectory distinction; then a 45–60 min goal → plan → bounded execution → measured feedback diagram; end with a 20–30 min requirements checkpoint. Proposed time ~140–185 min across remaining Week 1, adjustable within the weekly ~5 h ceiling; actual time already spent is unknown.
- **Q1 syllabus remains intact:** deep standalone FK/IK coding gate still Weeks 3–4, mechanical torque/FFF design Weeks 5–6, detailed Jacobian trajectory/singularity limits Weeks 9–10. The earlier Jacobian introduction is **not** equivalent to finishing the Weeks 9–10 practical gate.
- **Still open from requirements Issue #6:** physical tray/part dimensions, joint limits, collision geometry, motor/encoder choice, observed state and safe execution design. Illustrative 40×30×30 mm / 100 g part is not a learner-confirmed spec.

### Week 1 exit check

- Can sketch SCARA R-R-P-R, define each joint variable, explain 3D position + independent yaw, and distinguish gripper actuation from the four pose DOF.
- Can compute the unconstrained radial envelope and explain why real limits reduce it.
- Can justify at least three candidate requirements (part dimensions/mass, tray geometry, accuracy/repeatability, reach or Z travel) while marking unmeasured values as estimates.
- Can explain ROS 2 vs local controller responsibilities and identify one failure/safety scenario.
- A concise note captures design assumptions and unknowns. No premature assessment of professional engineering competency.

## Work model / evidence

Maintain **~2 hours engineering theory, 2 hours problem-solving/CAD/code/hands-on and 1 hour analysis/notes each week**. Limits are ~5 hours total; changes in pace are driven by comprehension and hardware readiness, not a list of software tools. Keep one theory issue + one implementation issue active at a time.

For learning claims record independent explanation, known conventions, actual numerical tests, reproducible commands/artifacts and limitations. `L0` means unassessed, not incapable.
